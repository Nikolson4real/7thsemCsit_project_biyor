# Dandi Biyo Official Rules and Regulations

## 1. Introduction

Dandi Biyo is a traditional Nepali sport that has been played for centuries. This document provides the official rules and regulations for standardized gameplay.

## 2. Equipment Specifications

### 2.1 The Dandi (Stick)

The Dandi is the longer stick used to strike the Biyo.

**Standard Specifications:**
- Length: 45-75 cm (recommended: 60 cm)
- Diameter: 2-3 cm
- Material: Wood (preferably bamboo or hardwood)
- Must be straight and cylindrical

### 2.2 The Biyo (Playing Piece)

The Biyo is the smaller wooden piece that is struck.

**Standard Specifications:**
- Length: 6-10 cm (recommended: 8 cm)
- Diameter: 1.5-3 cm (recommended: 2.5 cm)
- Shape: Cylindrical with tapered ends (spindle shape)
- Material: Hardwood
- Both ends must be sharpened to allow for striking

## 3. Playing Field Setup

### 3.1 Field Dimensions

**Standard Field:**
- Length: 25-40 meters (recommended: 30 meters)
- Width: 15-25 meters (recommended: 20 meters)
- Surface: Level ground (grass, dirt, or hard surface)

### 3.2 Anchor Point

**Definition:** The anchor point (dugout or striking circle) is where the Biyo is initially struck.

**Specifications:**
- Diameter: 30-50 cm circle
- Depth: 3-5 cm depression in the ground
- Location: Center of one end of the field

### 3.3 Boundary Lines

- Clear boundary markers must be established
- Out-of-bounds areas must be clearly marked
- The playing area is rectangular with defined limits

## 4. Game Rules

### 4.1 Number of Players

**Standard Game:**
- 2-4 players (individual play)
- OR 2 teams of 2-5 players each

**Team Composition:**
- Equal number of players per team
- Substitutions allowed between rounds

### 4.2 Striking the Biyo

#### 4.2.1 Initial Strike

1. Place the Biyo across the anchor point depression
2. One end of the Biyo must rest on the edge of the depression
3. Strike the raised end of the Biyo with the Dandi
4. The Biyo must become airborne

#### 4.2.2 Valid Strike Requirements

A strike is considered VALID when:
- The Biyo is struck while positioned correctly at the anchor
- The Biyo becomes airborne
- The Biyo lands within the playing field boundaries
- The striker does not step outside the anchor circle during the strike

#### 4.2.3 Invalid Strikes (Fouls)

A strike is considered INVALID (foul) when:
- The Biyo does not become airborne
- The Biyo lands out of bounds
- The striker steps outside the anchor circle
- The Dandi touches the ground during the strike
- The Biyo is struck more than once in a single attempt

**Penalty for Foul:**
- Loss of turn
- No points awarded
- Opposing player/team takes their turn

## 5. Scoring System

### 5.1 Basic Scoring Method

**Primary Rule:** Distance-based scoring using Dandi lengths

**Calculation:**
1. Measure the straight-line distance from the anchor point to where the Biyo landed
2. Measure this distance using the Dandi stick as the unit
3. Count the number of Dandi lengths
4. Each Dandi length = 1 point

**Example:**
- Biyo lands 15 meters from anchor
- Dandi length is 60 cm (0.6 meters)
- Distance in Dandi lengths: 15 ÷ 0.6 = 25
- Score: 25 points

### 5.2 Measurement Rules

- Measurements must be made immediately after the Biyo lands
- Distance is measured from the center of the anchor to the center of the Biyo's landing position
- The Dandi used for striking is also used for measurement
- Round down fractional Dandi lengths (e.g., 7.8 Dandi lengths = 7 points)

### 5.3 Bonus Points

**Mid-Air Strikes:**
- If the striker hits the Biyo again while it is airborne (after the initial strike): +10 bonus points
- This is highly skilled and rare
- Distance is measured from where the Biyo finally lands

**Boundary Bonus:**
- If the Biyo lands exactly on a boundary line: +5 bonus points
- Determined by official judgment

## 6. Game Flow and Turns

### 6.1 Turn Order

**Individual Play:**
1. Players take turns in rotation
2. Each player gets one strike per turn
3. Turns continue until a predetermined number of rounds

**Team Play:**
1. Teams alternate turns
2. Within a team, players rotate who strikes
3. All team members must strike before any player strikes again

### 6.2 Round Structure

**Standard Game:**
- Each player/team gets 5-10 turns (rounds)
- At the end of all rounds, highest total score wins

**Alternative - Target Score:**
- First to reach 100 points (or other agreed target) wins
- Game ends immediately when target is reached

### 6.3 Ties

If scores are tied at the end of regulation:
1. Sudden death: One strike each until tie is broken
2. Highest score in sudden death wins
3. If still tied, repeat

## 7. Field Conditions and Standardization

### 7.1 Surface Requirements

**Acceptable Surfaces:**
- Natural grass (preferred for traditional play)
- Dirt/soil (must be level and compact)
- Sand (only for casual play, not official)
- Concrete (only for urban play, affects scoring)

**Surface Maintenance:**
- Field must be level with no significant obstacles
- Remove stones, holes, or debris that could affect play
- Anchor point must be maintained throughout the game

### 7.2 Weather Conditions

**Acceptable Conditions:**
- Dry or slightly damp ground
- No active rain during play
- Wind: Game continues unless extreme

**Unacceptable Conditions:**
- Heavy rain or standing water
- Extreme wind that affects Biyo flight
- Unsafe ground conditions

## 8. Equipment Validation

### 8.1 Pre-Game Inspection

Before each game:
1. All Dandis must be measured and approved
2. All Biyos must be measured and approved
3. Equipment that does not meet specifications must be replaced
4. Each player's equipment must remain consistent throughout the game

### 8.2 Equipment Standardization Check

**Critical Checks:**
- Dandi length: 45-75 cm (60 cm recommended)
- Biyo length: 6-10 cm (8 cm recommended)
- Biyo must have properly tapered/sharpened ends
- No modifications that provide unfair advantage (e.g., weighted Biyo)

**Referee Judgment:**
- Referee has final say on equipment validity
- Players may challenge equipment before game starts
- Equipment disputes must be resolved before play begins

## 9. Fouls and Penalties

### 9.1 Common Fouls

1. **Stepping Foul:** Striker steps outside anchor circle
2. **Double Strike:** Striking the Biyo more than once (unless intentional mid-air strike)
3. **Ground Touch:** Dandi touches ground during strike
4. **Out of Bounds:** Biyo lands outside playing field
5. **Equipment Foul:** Using non-regulation equipment

### 9.2 Foul Consequences

**First Offense:**
- Loss of turn
- No points for that strike
- Warning issued

**Repeated Offenses (3+ fouls):**
- Disqualification from current round
- Possible game forfeit (referee's decision)

## 10. Dispute Resolution

### 10.1 Measurement Disputes

If players disagree on distance measurement:
1. Neutral party (referee or agreed-upon player) measures
2. Measurement is final
3. Use consistent measurement method throughout game

### 10.2 Boundary Calls

For ambiguous landing positions:
1. Referee makes judgment call
2. If no referee, majority vote of all players
3. When in doubt, call goes to defending team

### 10.3 Equipment Disputes

Any disputes about equipment:
1. Must be raised before the game starts
2. Referee inspects and makes ruling
3. Ruling is binding for entire game

## 11. Safety Guidelines

### 11.1 Player Safety

**Required Precautions:**
- Maintain safe distance from striker (minimum 3 meters)
- No spectators within 5 meters of anchor point
- Striker must announce before striking
- Players must be alert when Biyo is airborne

### 11.2 Equipment Safety

**Safety Checks:**
- Inspect Dandi for cracks or splinters before each game
- Biyo must have no sharp edges that could cause injury
- Replace damaged equipment immediately
- Do not use wet or slippery equipment

## 12. Advanced Play and Variations

### 12.1 Target Zones

**Optional Rule for Advanced Play:**
- Divide the field into zones (near, middle, far)
- Different point values for each zone
- Near zone: 1x multiplier
- Middle zone: 1.5x multiplier  
- Far zone: 2x multiplier

### 12.2 Obstacle Course Variation

- Place obstacles in the field
- Hitting obstacles results in bonus/penalty points
- More challenging and entertaining
- Not used in official competitions

## Appendix A: Quick Reference

**Standard Equipment:**
- Dandi: 60 cm long, 2-3 cm diameter
- Biyo: 8 cm long, 2.5 cm diameter, tapered ends

**Standard Field:**
- 30m x 20m rectangular playing area
- Anchor point: 30-50 cm circle at one end

**Standard Scoring:**
- 1 point per Dandi length of distance
- Measure from anchor to landing point
- Use the Dandi as measuring unit

**Common Fouls:**
- Stepping outside anchor
- Biyo doesn't go airborne
- Out of bounds landing
- Dandi touches ground

**Game Format:**
- 2-4 individual players OR 2 teams
- 5-10 rounds per game
- Highest total score wins
